Where: "L'essentiel" point 5 ("donne à `pam` une somme des dissimilarités un peu plus basse qu'AP, à k égal"), and B2 ch. 6 "Signal d'alerte" ("une somme des distances euclidiennes au carré légèrement plus basse qu'AP, à k = 3 comme à k = 9"). Problem: I reran it on standardised Iris with `negDistMat(r=2)` and `seed=1234`, and the result flips with the `pam` call. With the default `pam(x, 3)`, which uses Euclidean distance, the sum of squared distances is AP 147.62 and pam 148.55, so AP is lower at k = 3. pam is lower only when it receives the squared-distance matrix (`pam(as.dist(dist(x)^2), 3)` gives 146.68), or when the cost is measured in plain Euclidean distance (131.36 vs 131.44). The document puts a "negative result" in the headline but never fixes the setting it depends on. `methodologie.md:66` requires "la même dissimilarité (distance euclidienne au carré)", and the plan does not carry that into ch. 6.2 or script 02. A user who writes `pam(x, k)`, the obvious call, gets the opposite of the warning at k = 3 and an unfair comparison.
